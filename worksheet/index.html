Prompt: To what extent did the Founders create a Constitution that successfully protects citizens' natural rights (life, liberty, and property)?
Step 1Step 2Step 3Step 4 draft

On the sheet, write it in your own words. Use these as the model. The facts and the structure are what matter, and they're what you'll need on Monday.

Step 1: analyze the prompt

Rewrite the prompt in your own words so you know exactly what it's asking.

How well did the people who wrote the Constitution build a government that actually protects everyone's rights to life, liberty and property?

Why: Extent means how much, so the answer is never just yes or no. Founders is who. Constitution is what. Successfully protects is what you're judging. Natural rights are rights everyone is born with (John Locke): life, liberty and property.

Step 2: the three options

"To what extent" prompts are a spectrum. Name the three places you could land.

  • Option 1, to a great extent: the Founders built strong protections (separation of powers, checks and balances, the Bill of Rights).
  • Option 2, to a limited extent: they protected natural rights, but only for some people (white men with property).
  • Option 3, very little: they failed, because the Constitution protected slavery and denied millions their rights.

Why: The best essays land between two options and explain why. That's where the complexity points on the rubric come from. We argue to a large extent, but not for everyone at first, between Option 1 and Option 2.

Step 3: gather evidence

Collect evidence for every side before you pick one. Each row: the evidence, then which option it supports and why.

At first mostly white men who owned property could vote. Women, enslaved people and Native Americans were left out.		Option 2. Rights were protected well, but only for some citizens.
Option 5The Constitution was built to grow
Article V lets it be amended: the 13th (1865) ended slavery, the 14th (1868) says no state can take "life, liberty, or property, without due process of law" and guarantees equal protection, the 15th (1870) banned racial voting limits. Marbury v. Madison (1803) gave courts judicial review to enforce rights.		Option 1, with a limit. The Founders' design let protection reach everyone over time, even though it didn't at first.

Why: Rows 1, 2 and 5 support your thesis. Rows 3 and 4 become your alternative perspective. Gathering the other side first is what lets you beat it later.

Step 4: draft your response

Use TEA in every body paragraph: Topic sentence, Evidence, Analysis.

Context

Background that shows why the topic matters: who, what, where, when, why. At least 3 connected sentences before the thesis.

After the Revolution, Americans had just fought a war against a king they believed had violated their natural rights. The Articles of Confederation that followed created a government too weak to protect anyone, so in 1787 delegates met in Philadelphia to write a new Constitution. Inspired by John Locke, who argued that governments exist to protect life, liberty and property, the Founders had to build a government strong enough to protect those rights but limited enough that it would never threaten them.

Why it works: Broad to narrow: the Revolution, then the weak Articles, then Philadelphia 1787 and Locke. Three connected sentences that lead straight into the thesis.

Thesis

Answer the prompt with a claim, then outline both body paragraphs. [Claim] because [Argument 1] and [Argument 2].

The Founders created a Constitution that protected citizens' natural rights to a large extent, because they divided power so no branch could become a tyranny and added a Bill of Rights that directly protects life, liberty and property. However, at first those protections only fully covered some Americans.

Why it works: Claim (to a large extent), argument 1 (divided power), argument 2 (Bill of Rights), plus a "however" that shows complexity and sets up the alternative perspective.

Evidence 1 (TEA)

Topic sentence, then evidence, then analysis.

T: One major reason the Constitution protects natural rights is that the Founders divided power so no single group could take them away. E: Following Montesquieu, Articles I, II and III split the government into a legislative, an executive and a judicial branch. Each branch can check the others: the President can veto laws, Congress can override a veto with 2/3 of both houses, and the Senate must confirm judges. A: This matters because tyranny, the thing the colonists had just fought against, happens when one person or group holds all the power. By making the branches compete, the Founders made it very hard for any leader to take away citizens' liberty, which shows they protected natural rights through the structure itself.

Why it works: The analysis says why the evidence matters (it prevents tyranny) and ties it back to the prompt (natural rights). That's the part most people skip.

Evidence 2 (TEA)

Topic sentence, then evidence, then analysis.

T: Another reason is that the Bill of Rights directly protects life, liberty and property. E: When the Antifederalists refused to ratify without it, the Founders added the first ten amendments in 1791. The 1st Amendment protects speech and religion, the 4th bans unreasonable searches and seizures, and the 5th says no person can be "deprived of life, liberty, or property, without due process of law." A: The 5th Amendment uses almost the exact words of Locke's natural rights, which proves the Founders meant to protect them. Writing these rights down gave citizens a guarantee they could use against their own government, so the Constitution protects natural rights in its text, not just in theory.

Why it works: Quoting the 5th Amendment is your strongest move: the prompt says life, liberty and property, and so does the Constitution.

Alternative perspective (TEA)

Topic sentence, the other side's evidence, then why your argument is still stronger.

T: Critics argue that the Founders did not successfully protect natural rights, because those rights were only protected for some Americans. E: The Constitution protected slavery through the Three Fifths Compromise and the Fugitive Slave Clause, and it allowed the slave trade until 1808. At first, women, enslaved people and Native Americans also could not vote. A: This is true to an extent, because millions of people were denied their natural rights. However, the Founders also wrote Article V so the Constitution could change. The 13th Amendment (1865) ended slavery, and the 14th (1868) said no state can take anyone's "life, liberty, or property, without due process of law." The system they designed was eventually used to extend natural rights to everyone, which is why their Constitution succeeded to a large extent.

Why it works: "True to an extent. However..." admits the other side is real, then shows why your side wins. That's what the rubric wants.

Conclusion (not on the sheet, but needed in the essay)

Thesis in new words, then why it matters.

The Founders protected life, liberty and property to a large extent by dividing power and writing a Bill of Rights, even though those protections first reached only some Americans. Because they built the Constitution to be amended, the same document that once allowed slavery later became the tool used to protect everyone's natural rights.

Why it works: New words for the thesis plus one sentence about why it matters today.
