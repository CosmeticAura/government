Key terms sheet

Copy these by hand onto your prep sheet and hand it in Monday before the essay. It counts toward Criterion B. Reword anything so it sounds like you.

Key term, concept or exampleExplanation in your own wordsHow it connects to a driving question
Natural rightsRights everyone is born with that government can't take away, like life, liberty and property (Locke)Founders' goals: the Declaration says government exists to protect these
Social contractPeople give up some freedom to a government, and in return it protects their rights. If it stops, the people can change itFounders' goals: an Enlightenment idea behind "consent of the governed"
Popular sovereigntyPower comes from the people, which is why the Constitution starts with "We the People"Founders' goals and evolution: more people got a voice over time (15th, 19th, 26th amendments)
Federalists vs AntifederalistsFederalists (Hamilton, Madison) wanted a strong national government after the weak Articles of Confederation. Antifederalists feared it would abuse its powerFounders' goals: their debate shaped what the Constitution became
Bill of RightsThe first 10 amendments (1791), added to protect individual liberties so the Antifederalists would agree to ratifyFounders' goals and 21st century: still protects speech and privacy today
Checks and balancesEach branch can limit the others, like a veto, a 2/3 override, or the Senate confirming judges, so no branch gets too much powerFounders' goals and 21st century: comes from Montesquieu's separation of powers
Amendment processArticle V: 2/3 of both houses propose, then 3/4 of the states ratify. Only 27 of 11,000+ proposed amendments passedEvolution: how the Constitution changes, slowly and with wide support
13th AmendmentEnded slavery in 1865, "except as a punishment for crime"Evolution and 21st century: that loophole connects to mass incarceration (13th documentary)
14th AmendmentCitizenship for everyone born here, plus equal protection and due process from the states (1868)Evolution: used to apply the Bill of Rights to the states
15th AmendmentVoting can't be denied because of race (1870)Evolution: Jim Crow blocked it until the Voting Rights Act of 1965
4th AmendmentNo unreasonable searches or seizures without a warrant21st century: AI and surveillance, police now need a warrant for phone data
Commerce ClauseCongress can regulate trade between states (Article I)21st century: the power Congress would use to regulate AI
Supremacy ClauseFederal law beats conflicting state law (Article VI)21st century: state vs federal AI laws, and Federal vs California on emissions
Judicial reviewCourts can strike down laws that go against the Constitution. It came from Marbury v. Madison (1803), not the textEvolution and 21st century: how courts protect rights and apply old rules to new tech

Driving questions: the founders' goals and priorities; how American democracy has evolved; to what extent the Constitution serves Americans in the 21st century.
